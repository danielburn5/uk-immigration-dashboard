A note about this project

Why I built this

I wanted to look beyond the headlines and understand what the UK's migration figures actually say.

I started this project after seeing a graphic that put two real UK migration figures side by side: British citizens leaving and non-EU nationals arriving. The numbers were accurate, but the suggestion behind them went much further than the evidence.

It made me wonder how often we see migration statistics presented without the context needed to understand them. So I built this site to look at the trends, check specific claims and make the underlying figures easier to explore.

The aim isn't to argue for one political side. It's to show what the data supports, what it contradicts and what it simply can't tell us.

Immigration is a legitimate subject for debate. I just think that debate is better when we can separate the facts from the assumptions. You don't have to agree with me to use this site—or to tell me where I've got something wrong.

Sources and limitations

The figures come from the ONS November 2026 long-term international migration release and its accompanying spreadsheet. They describe migration over 12-month periods ending in June, not calendar years.

Estimates can be revised, and methodology changes around 2021 make some historical comparisons less reliable, especially for British citizens. These figures alone can't establish people's motives or the wider effects of migration.

This is an independent personal project, not an ONS publication or endorsement.
