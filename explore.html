Migration over time

Each year shows the number of people who moved to or from the UK during the 12 months ending that June.

About the figures

These are ONS estimates of people moving for at least 12 months, not counts of every border crossing. The estimates are rounded, and changes in ONS methods around 2021 can make earlier and later years harder to compare directly.

How many people are arriving and leaving?

UK-wide figures, 2012–2026. The chart labels use “k” for thousands.

Immigration has almost halved since 2023. Arrivals fell from 1.47 million to 738,000. After accounting for departures, net migration was 77,000 in 2026.

Are more British people leaving?

British nationals moving to or from the UK, 2022–2026. “k” means thousand.

British departures have remained broadly stable. Fewer British nationals arriving explains most of the growing net loss.

Where are arrivals from?

People moving to the UK, grouped by nationality, 2021–2026.

About three in four people arriving in 2026 were non-EU+ nationals. Nationality is not the same as ethnicity.

Why do non-EU+ nationals come to the UK?

Why people from outside the EU+ group initially moved to the UK. “k” means thousand.

Work and study account for 392,000 of 550,000 non-EU+ arrivals in 2026, including dependants. Asylum accounts for 73,000 (about 13%). These are reasons for immigration, not assessments of people's character or contribution.

About these figures

These are numbers of migrants, not rates relative to the population of each age group. They do not measure employment, income or motivations. Age estimates are rounded, so totals may differ slightly.

Source: ONS November 2026 publication, Tables 7a and 7d. The chart shows only the two nationality groups available here, not everyone who migrates. Read the ONS release.

What do the numbers prove?

These data show how many people move, their nationality groups, ages and initial reasons for moving. They cannot on their own establish cause, economic contribution or desirability.
