The trends behind the debate

All graphs show long-term international migration in rolling 12-month periods ending in June. Figures are ONS estimates, rounded to the nearest thousand.

How many people are arriving and leaving?

All nationalities · years ending June 2012–2026 · thousands of people

What stands out: Immigration peaked at 1.466m in 2023 and then halved by 2026. Net migration fell from 924k to 77k over the same period. Methodology changes around 2021 limit comparisons across that boundary.

Are more British people leaving?

British nationals · years ending June 2022–2026 · thousands

What stands out: Departures were broadly flat (240k–257k). Arrivals fell from 172k to 109k, widening the net loss.

Where are arrivals from?

Arrivals by nationality group · years ending June 2021–2026 · thousands

What stands out: Non-EU+ nationals accounted for 550k of 738k arrivals in 2026 (about 75%). This measures nationality groups, not ethnicity or individual circumstances.

Why do non-EU+ nationals come to the UK?

Long-term arrivals by initial immigration reason · thousands

Work and study account for 392,000 of 550,000 non-EU+ arrivals in 2026, including dependants. Asylum accounts for 73,000 (about 13%). These are reasons for immigration, not assessments of people's character or contribution.

What do the numbers prove?

These data show how many people move, their nationality groups, ages and initial reasons for moving. They cannot on their own establish cause, economic contribution or desirability.
